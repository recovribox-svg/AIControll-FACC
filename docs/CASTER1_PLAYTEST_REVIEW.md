# Caster1 playtest review

The review covers all three AI logs, the combat log and the author's observations. [Input fingerprints and accounting](CASTER1_PLAYTEST_INPUTS.json) identify the captures; raw logs remain private. TB1's startup MVID is caster1 `35b96ebd-8929-4ce8-baa4-564d03bc454f`. TB2 and RT contain no independent startup marker. This task starts at caster1 packaging `f278114` over development `1f58150`; PR #33 remains its prerequisite. Remote development was fetched before editing and remained `1f58150`.

There are **13 fights and 1,043 logged decision summaries**: TB1 2/194, TB2 6/560 and RT 5/289. Summaries include end-turn/wait choices and retries; they are not a count of every driver invocation. The combat log also contains 13 combat starts. The captures change party composition and Magus level, so they cannot establish a timing improvement against one another.

## Confirmed faults repaired here

**Multi-projectile damage was counted once.** Five `DmgCheck` rows report Drow Magus's Scorching Ray at 27.3–33.7 damage against a 15-point estimate (TB2 1457/2815/4611, RT 446/1363). The old estimator reads one damage payload and never the root delivery's projectile count. Native `AbilityDeliverProjectile.Deliver` takes the selected rank's number of projectiles, bounded by its actual array, and applies that payload for each. At caster level 7 the supplied Scorching Ray fires two rays. Magic Missile has the same delivery mechanism.

The shared estimator now reads that native rank from its existing damage context, applies mitigation and cast bonuses per payload, and sums the selected simple deliveries. No extra contexts, actors, projectiles or rule events are created. Rank/array metadata replaces a spell-name or caster-level table. Cone, line, controlled-projectile, Hand of the Apprentice and fusion appraisal keep their separate contracts. Ricochet delivery-layer estimates remain one layer. Actual ray attack rolls also separate damage-check samples: two landed rays are compared with two payload estimates, and a missed sibling cannot invalidate a landed ray. Multiple damage actions sharing one ray's attack roll remain one sample. Native fixtures cover Scorching Ray, Magic Missile, Hellfire Ray, rank boundaries and array limits.

**A critical Pure Mage heal was refused after its safe approach.** TB1 1874–1889: Orvath chooses Cure Moderate Wounds on the severely injured Magus, successfully approaches, then both that heal and Cure Light Wounds are refused as a physical action. The fallback is a low-value summon. Selection reads the native `UnitUseAbility.NeedLoS`; the final Pure Mage check hardcoded `true`. The actual sticky-touch source implements `ILineOfSightIgnore`. The candidate now carries its native sight flag through the final range check. Existing critical-heal, moved-recipient and safe-approach requirements remain. Path generation and movement policy are unchanged.

**Serialized native burn settings forced an incorrect legacy consequence model.** Eleven notices, first TB2 148, identify PsychokineticistBurnBuff -> BurnNumberProperty -> native KineticistBurnPropertyGetter **with settings**. Presence of that object alone caused rejection and HP/nonlethal fallback. Native `PropertyValueGetter.GetValue` actually applies `PropertySettings.Apply` to the burn getter's base value before the surrounding rank calculation. The reader now uses that actor-free native method in the same order, including native division, negation and limits. Existing held-buff ranks and decision-local delta caching remain. Optional composite/provider contracts remain bounded; malformed settings retain the visible fallback. The logs do not serialize Orvath's settings fields, so a particular penalty amount is not inferred from this notice or from an archetype name. Native graph clones verify default settings, division plateaus/boundaries, negation/limits and invalid arithmetic.

## Movement findings: proposal only

Twenty-five of the 27 explicit TB issue refusals concern paths/reach/sight/full-attack movement; two are the repaired heal guard. Examples include repeated `+5ft` choices ending 0.5–1.4 m short (TB2 3313–3556), a long outlier 17.5 m short (TB1 1533), and a refused path into Corrosive Bomb (TB2 2121). The dispatcher safely refuses them, but the repeated candidate failures waste decisions and expose disagreement between candidate geometry and actual routes.

RT 1899–1971 repeatedly selects short exits from Brimorak smoke. Path-inspection endpoints are on the ground while the requested points retain a higher Y coordinate; several endpoint gaps are 1.8–2.8 m. This is evidence for investigating destination projection and the actual post-walk hazard boundary. It does not prove which single movement change is correct. Camellia and Bandit also have two stuck-walk cancellations in RT.

A bounded next proposal is to validate candidate full-attack approaches against the same native route/range/sight checks used by the dispatcher, reuse those results within one decision, and validate hazard exits at the native endpoint. Before changing code, establish whether the repeated smoke choices are caused by projection, moving areas, vertical hazard membership or interrupted paths. Preserve the current action and AoO restrictions. **No movement, commitment, retreat, pathfinding or stuck-cancellation changes are made by this review.** These proposals require the author's explicit approval.

## Performance and verification

There are 87 logged slow samples: 22 TB1, 32 TB2 and 33 RT. Seven explicitly report memory cleanup, including the 1,406 ms RT Tuvel sample with heap **-324,644 KB**. The slowest ability at that sample costs 13.8 ms; attributing the enclosing pause to that ability would be misleading. Some non-cleanup Orvath/Tuvel decisions still cost hundreds of milliseconds with large spell/recipient sets. Stage heap deltas measure net live-heap changes, rather than exact allocation or responsibility for a collection.

The repairs add cached root metadata and constant-time rank/flag reads. Burn settings reuse existing per-decision delta caches. They add no candidate search, global actor cache, simulated cast or extra scoring context. Native coverage and real-reference compilation establish API/metadata correctness; matched Unity timing and live cast execution remain gameplay checks. The final receipt records the checked source, test counts, strict compatible ProfileAudit and original full-corpus enum failure separately.

Replay both TB and RT: Scorching Ray at CL 3/7/11 (including a miss and fire resistance), Magic Missile at several rank boundaries, a critical ally heal after a safe Pure Mage approach, and a paid Orvath blast whose supported consequence is a save penalty. Ordinary Ranged martial attacks, Reach touches, bombs, fusion payloads, native resistance and existing swarm behavior should retain their rules.
